# F10 - behaviour under dependency failure

_Run 2026-09-08 on the `lab` stand, host **`epyc32`** (see
`machine-spec-epyc32-2026-09-08.md` - 32 threads, 122 GiB, KVM guest).
Scenario `scenarios/f10-dependency-failure.sh` (#2978, epic #2840),
unmodified. Branch `perf-programme-2840` @ `4ff884d8e`.
Run groups: `run1788877883` (baseline + 12 faults), `run1788890881` (`I3`
retry - see §6). **All 13 arms returned `status=VALID`.**_

---

## 1. The headline: orders are lost silently when the DESTINATION fails

Every arm pushes **12 orders** through the real ingestion path. The table is
the whole finding.

| arm | fault | `marketplace.order.sync` jobs | max attempts | deferred ms | orders that never reached the destination |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `baseline` | none (proxy transparent) | 12 succeeded/**ok** | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| `D1` | PrestaShop 500 on a fraction | 12 succeeded/**ok** | **0** | **0** | **5 of 12** |
| `D2a` | PrestaShop 429 **with** `Retry-After` | 12 succeeded/**ok** | **0** | **0** | **5 of 12** |
| `D2b` | PrestaShop 429 **without** `Retry-After` | 12 succeeded/**ok** | **0** | **0** | **11 of 12** |
| `D3` | connection held past client timeout | 12 succeeded/**ok** | **0** | **0** | **1 of 12** |
| `D4` | cart succeeds, `importorder` fails | 12 succeeded/**ok** | **0** | **0** | **12 of 12** |
| `M1` | source times out during hydration | 12 succeeded/ok | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| `M2` | source returns malformed payload | 12 succeeded/ok | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| `M2t` | source returns truncated payload | 12 succeeded/ok | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| `M3` | source rejects the quantity write | n/a (quantity path) | 0 | 0 | n/a |
| `I1` | Redis slow (blocked client) | 12 succeeded/ok | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| `I2` | Redis drops entirely | 9 ok, 2 running, 1 queued | 0 | 0 | 2 |
| `I3` | worker SIGKILLed mid-job | 10 ok, **2 running** | 0 | 0 | 0 |

All **measured**, from each arm's own `ledger-after-recovery.json` and
`fault-observations.txt`.

**Five destination-failure arms, and in every one of them the job ledger says
the work succeeded while the order did not arrive.** `D4` is the extreme: 12
of 12 orders never reached PrestaShop and 12 of 12 jobs report
`succeeded / outcome: ok`. This is `OrderSyncService.dispatchToDestinations`'s
`Promise.allSettled` swallowing the per-destination failure - the exact defect
`post_guard_destination_creates` was built to see, reproduced under five
distinct fault shapes.

### The loss is PERMANENT, not delayed

Checked against the live database **2.5 hours after the last fault was
removed**, over the orders created during F10's arms:

```
orders created in the F10 window : 131
  carrying a failed syncStatus   :  34
  STILL with no PrestaShop syncedAt : 36
```

Nothing re-drove them. Nothing could: `attempts_max` is **0** on every arm,
so the jobs never retried - they had already declared success. An operator
gets no failed job, no dead row, no counter, and 36 orders that exist in
OpenLinker and do not exist in the shop.

### `Retry-After` is ignored, and `deferredTotalMs` is 0 everywhere

`D2a` sends `429` **with** a `Retry-After` header - precisely the input
#2613's penalty-free deferral exists to consume. Measured
`deferred_ms_total: 0` and `attempts_max: 0`: the order path neither deferred
nor retried. `D2b` (429 **without** the header) is more than twice as
destructive (11 of 12 vs 5 of 12), so the header is not merely unused - the
difference in loss between the two arms is the shop's own behaviour, and
OpenLinker responds identically to both.

## 2. The asymmetry: the SOURCE side is protected, the DESTINATION side is not

The `M` arms are the control, and they came out the opposite way.

`M1`'s fault was genuinely applied - not assumed. From the arm's own log:

```
fault installed: {"injected":true,"injector":"allegro-stub",
  "rule":{"mode":"timeout","holdMs":35000,"endpoints":["checkout"],"fraction":1}}
```

`fraction: 1` = every checkout hydration held for 35 s. The effect is visible
in the sampler for the whole 90 s window - the queue simply stops draining:

```
t+10s queued=11 running=2 dead=0
...
t+90s queued=10 running=2 dead=0
```

And after the fault lifted, **all 12 finished `succeeded/ok` with nothing
lost.** Same for `M2` (malformed) and `M2t` (truncated).

So the two directions behave in opposite ways:

- **Source fails** -> jobs BLOCK, the queue backs up, throughput drops to
  ~zero, and every order completes correctly once the source returns. Slow,
  lossless, visible in the queue depth.
- **Destination fails** -> jobs report success IMMEDIATELY, the queue drains
  normally, and the orders are gone. Fast, lossy, invisible.

The failure mode an operator can see is the harmless one.

## 3. Infrastructure: nothing recovered without a human inside the observation window

Both Redis-loss and worker-kill arms left work stranded, and F10's own
recovery probe says so rather than inferring recovery:

| arm | `recoveredWithoutAHumanAfterSecs` | observed for | still in flight at end |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| `I2` (Redis drops) | **null** | 240 s | 10 |
| `I3` (worker SIGKILL) | **null** | 240 s | 2 |

`null` means *not observed to recover within 240 s* - it is explicitly **not**
a claim that recovery never happens, and the artefact's own `note` field says
so. The likely reason for `I3` is structural rather than mysterious:
`StuckJobRecoveryService` requeues on a **stale `lockedAt`** with a
15-minute threshold, so a 240 s observation cannot see it by construction. The
two `running` rows are a worker killed while holding its claim.

**`I2` additionally killed something F10 did not report** - `lab-api` died on
an unhandled Redis `error` event and stayed dead for 3 hours, and the arm was
still certified `VALID` because `post_guard_containers_stable` compares
`StartedAt` and a crashed container does not change it. That is written up in
full as Finding 8 of `campaign-notes-epyc32-2026-09-08.md`, because it is a
guard gap and an availability finding rather than an F10 measurement.

## 4. `M3` measured nothing about orders, by design

`M3` is the only arm on the quantity-write path, so its
`marketplace.order.sync` count is legitimately 0 and its
destination-creates observation is legitimately `ok`. It is reported here as
not-applicable rather than as a pass.

## 5. Why every arm is `VALID` even where a post-guard objected

`D1`'s `fault-observations.txt` contains
`DISCARDED post_guard_destination_creates: 5 order(s) ...` while its
`verdict.txt` reads `VALID`. That is correct and deliberate: F10 records each
guard's observation as evidence but does not let the destination-creates guard
veto an arm whose entire subject is a destination that fails. A failed order
is the measurement here, not contamination of it.

The consequence for reading this report: **the `VALID` verdicts certify that
the WINDOW was clean (no stray attempts, no limiter degradation, the offered
load reached the queue), not that OpenLinker behaved well.** The behaviour is
the table in §1.

## 6. `I3` ran separately, and why

On the first run `I3` aborted in pre-flight:

```
FATAL guard_scheduler_off: lab-worker-1 has OL_SCHEDULER_ENABLED=true
```

The guard was right to refuse - a cron firing into the window would
contaminate it. The provenance of that `true` is **not established**: F10
touches the flag nowhere (zero grep hits), `docker-compose.lab.yml` defaults
it to `false`, and F10 restarts the worker with `docker start`, which cannot
change a container's environment. It read `false` afterwards and the retry
passed the same guard. Recorded as unexplained rather than papered over.

`I3` was then re-run alone (`--faults=I3`), `VALID`, on an otherwise
identical stand. Its numbers sit in run group `run1788890881`; every other
arm is `run1788877883`.

The first run also left the stand broken - `perf-prestashop.config.baseUrl`
still pointed at the by-then-deleted fault proxy, because the teardown
reaches the api over HTTP and the api was dead. F10 printed the exact repair
command rather than claiming success. Both that and a silently-lost
`perf-woocommerce.enabledCapabilities` were repaired by hand before the
retry; see Finding 8.

## 7. What this run did NOT establish

- **Whether the lost orders are recoverable at all.** The operator-facing
  remedy (the destination Retry action) was never exercised - this run only
  establishes that nothing automatic re-drove them in 2.5 hours.
- **The recovery time for `I2` / `I3`.** Both exceed the 240 s observation.
  The `StuckJobRecoveryService` 15-minute threshold is read from code, not
  measured here.
- **Anything at scale.** 12 orders per arm on an otherwise idle stand. These
  are correctness-under-failure results, not capacity-under-failure results.
- **Real marketplace or real shop failure modes.** Faults are injected by a
  local proxy and a local stub; a real Allegro or a real hosted PrestaShop
  may fail in shapes not covered by these 12.
- **Repeat agreement.** One run per arm.
- **`M3`'s own subject** beyond "it did not touch the order path".
