# F8 - the realtime lane's cap sweep, and why it does NOT retire ADR-050's gap

_Sweep run 2026-09-09 on the `lab` stand (`results/f8-lane-caps/run1788945661-*`),
read and assessed 2026-09-10. Scenario `scenarios/f8-lane-caps.sh` (epic #2840)._

> **STATUS: four VALID arms, and the lane stays ILLUSTRATIVE.** That is not a
> contradiction and § 3 is the whole point of this report: the curve is real,
> and it does not answer the question ADR-050 asks.

---

## 1. What the sweep measured

`marketplace.offerQuantity.update` - the `realtime` lane's highest-volume real
member - 240 jobs on one connection scope, at four per-scope cap settings, with
the cap applied by recreating the worker between arms. Image sha `1f9a52d1`,
lane caps recorded per arm.

The scenario deliberately does NOT drive `marketplace.order.sync`, and its own
header explains why: `OrderSyncService` fans an ingested order out to every
`OrderProcessorManager` connection, so a 400-job arm would create 400 real
orders in a real PrestaShop against a declared 60 req/min. F4 already measured
that - both arms DISCARDED on `post_guard_limiter_degraded`, with the shop as
the ceiling rather than the lane. That substitution is a correction to ADR-050's
wording, not a shortcut around it.

## 2. The curve

| per-scope cap | span | jobs/s | exec mean | exec p95 | e2e p95 |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 244.1 s | 0.98 | 136 ms | 140 ms | 226.0 s |
| 2 | 122.3 s | 1.96 | 142 ms | 149 ms | 109.9 s |
| 4 | 55.4 s | 4.34 | 128 ms | 165 ms | 46.2 s |
| 8 | 20.7 s | **11.61** | **107 ms** | 178 ms | 13.7 s |

240 of 240 succeeded at every setting. Zero dead, zero stuck.

Two things in that table are worth stating plainly. **Per-job execution time does
not degrade** - it moves 136 -> 142 -> 128 -> 107 ms, i.e. it is flat and the
lowest figure is at the WIDEST cap, so nothing in the runner's own slot
machinery costs anything up to 8. And **throughput goes super-linear from 4 to
8** (4.34 -> 11.61, a 2.7x gain for a 2x cap), which is the signal that the span
at cap 8 is no longer measuring steady-state throughput: 240 jobs at ~110 ms
drain in twenty seconds, so fixed start-up and drain overhead is a large
fraction of the window.

## 3. Why this does not make the cap "measured"

ADR-050 names the missing realtime measurement as *"a saturation run: many
concurrent `marketplace.order.sync` against one destination, finding the
concurrency at which per-order latency degrades"*. This sweep finds no
degradation up to 8 - which is not the same as finding the knee. Three reasons,
and the scenario itself supplies the evidence for all three.

**The destination latency was the UNMEASURED FALLBACK, and the manifest says so.**
Every arm records `f8.stubConfig.latency`:

    "quantityMs": 120,
    "quantityMsMeasured": false

The scenario's own header defines that flag: it *"tells a reader whether a
driver set it deliberately or it fell back to the generic 120 ms default that
has no #2861 sandbox measurement behind it"*. It fell back. So the curve is
taken against a destination whose answer time is not a measured property of
anything.

**Only ONE latency was swept, and the scenario is built to need two.** Its header
states the falsifiable claim: *"if the knee moves with destination latency, then
a single global default cannot be 'the measured value' for this lane, and the
honest deliverable is guidance plus a range."* There is one summary CSV
(`run1788945661`); the earlier `run1788944358` left three arm directories, one
of them with an empty `verdict.txt`, and no summary at all. The claim is
therefore neither confirmed nor falsified.

**A cap bounds worker slots, never the destination.** Already recorded in the
architecture overview and measured by F7 (#2852): a probe sharing a saturated
connection ran at 5.5x its normal execution time. At 107 ms per job this arm's
destination is a stub answering instantly and fanning out to nothing, so the
curve says what OpenLinker's runner will admit and nothing about what a
marketplace will accept.

One further provenance note: `f8.stubConfig.runId` reads `f10-I2-1788938005`, so
the stub was still carrying an F10 arm's configuration when this sweep ran.
Harmless for the latency figure, which is recorded verbatim either way, but it
means the stub state was inherited rather than set for this run.

## 4. What would close the gap

A sweep at two DELIBERATELY SET quantity latencies, with
`quantityMsMeasured: true` in both manifests, run wide enough that per-job
execution time actually rises. If the knee sits at the same cap in both, the
lane has a measured default; if it moves, ADR-050 gets guidance plus a range
instead, which is the answer the scenario was built to produce.

**Until then `realtime` keeps 4/2 and stays labelled illustrative.** Nothing in
this report licenses raising it, and the flat exec-time column is the most
likely thing to be misread as licence - which is why it is stated here rather
than left in a CSV.

## 5. Limits

- One latency, and that latency unmeasured (§ 3).
- One connection scope, so the lane's TOTAL cap is never reached and its
  fairness argument is untested - the same gap `bulk` still has.
- `occMaxProxy` / `occMeanProxy` are reported and are NOT used for sizing:
  F4 § 2 records that the `sync_jobs`-row proxy over-reads, because a lane slot
  is released in-process when the handler resolves while the row stays
  `running` until a separate terminal write commits.
- The `fiscal` arm of this scenario is a separate matter and is measured
  elsewhere - see `results-F14-2026-09-10.md` § 4 and ADR-050 § Amendment
  (#2840), which took its figure from #3006's provider-latency sweep rather
  than from this scenario.
