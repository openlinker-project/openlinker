# F10 - behaviour under dependency failure, re-measured on the merged epic (2026-09-09)

Tree `1f9a52d1b`. `guard_build ok (image sha = tree HEAD, product paths
identical)`. Scenario start 2026-09-09T07:42:51Z. 14 windows attempted
(baseline + 13 injected faults); **13 carry `status=VALID`**, I3 carries none.
Every fault's installation was confirmed (`"injected":true`) except baseline,
which correctly reports `injected:false`.

## Headline: the third outcome did not occur on any arm

F10 asks one thing per fault - **is the order LOST, RETRIED, or SILENTLY
MARKED DONE?** - and the third is the finding worth hunting.

**`claimedButAbsentFromPsOrders = 0` on all 13 arms, with `readable = 1` on all
13.** No arm produced an order OpenLinker recorded as `synced` against an
`externalOrderId` naming no row in the shop. Because the shop was readable in
every window, those are **measured zeros**, not the detector's "nothing was
established" zero - a distinction the ledger's own note insists on and this
report honours.

## The one arm that did not recover: a total Redis outage

I2 stops and restarts `lab-redis` 35 s into the window. Its ledger:

- 12 child jobs enqueued for 12 pushed orders (`offered_work` confirms the
  intended load reached the queue)
- **4** `order_records` created, of which 2 synced and **2 carry no
  `syncStatus` entry at all** - neither synced nor failed
- `recoveredWithoutAHumanAfterSecs = null` over
  `recoveryObservedForSecs = 240`, with
  **`jobsStillInFlightWhenObservationEnded = 12`**
- `post_guard_containers_stable` refused and is correctly classified
  `EXPECTED-BY-FAULT`, so the window stays VALID

**The failure mode is STALLED, not silently lost.** Nothing was claimed done -
the two orders with no `syncStatus` are visibly unfinished, and
`claimedButAbsentFromPsOrders` is 0 here as everywhere. That is the good half.

**The bad half is that the api process died and stayed dead.** `lab-api`
exited **code 1** at 2026-09-09T08:51:29Z - inside I2's window - with an
unhandled `error` event on a Redis `Commander` instance propagating out of
`RedisSocket._RedisSocket_onSocketError` and terminating Node. It was still
down 40 minutes later, and F10 ran its remaining arm against a dead api.

Two things bound that finding and both are stated rather than implied:

1. **The container staying dead is the stand's configuration, not a product
   claim.** `docker-compose.lab.yml:381` sets `restart: 'no'` deliberately, so
   a crash is observable instead of masked. A deployment with a restart policy
   would restart it; how quickly, and what it would lose, is not measured here.
2. **The crash is a product behaviour, and it contrasts with a neighbour.**
   OpenLinker's rate limiter has a designed degraded mode for exactly this
   condition - it logs `rate_limiter_degraded_entered` and falls back to
   per-process in-memory limiting. So one Redis consumer degrades gracefully
   and another takes the process down. The sharp finding is the asymmetry, not
   the outage.

## A destination failure is visible on the order and invisible in the job ledger

On D1, D2a, D2b and D4 the destination genuinely failed for some orders, and
the shop's own row count matches the synced count exactly every time (6/6,
8/8, 6/6, 3/3) - so the accounting is honest at the order grain.

**But every one of those windows reports `status=succeeded, outcome=ok` on all
12 `marketplace.order.sync` jobs, with `attempts_max = 0`.** D1 failed 6 of 12
destinations while its job ledger records twelve clean successes and not one
retry.

This is the shape F10's own header names as a defect already met twice in this
campaign - a per-destination failure swallowed by the fan-out's
`Promise.allSettled` while the job records `ok`. **It is not silent loss**: the
failure IS recorded, on the order's `syncStatus`. What it means is narrower and
still worth acting on: **a job-level view - the Jobs dashboard, a job-outcome
alert - cannot see these failures at all.** An operator watching jobs would
have seen a clean window while half the orders never reached the shop.

## D4 leaves real garbage in the shop: 9 orphaned carts

D4 fails `controller=importorder` at 100% while letting the cart succeed.
Ground truth: **`psCartsCreated = 12`, `psOrdersCreated = 3`** - nine carts
created at PrestaShop that produced no order. The ledger's own note calls this
out as "work done at the destination that produced no order".

OpenLinker records nine failures and claims nothing, so this is not a
correctness failure in OL's accounting. It is a **cleanup** gap: the create
path is not transactional across cart-then-order, and nothing removes the cart
when the second half fails.

## Two arms whose result cannot be interpreted from these artefacts

**M2 (malformed, 50%) and M2t (truncated, 50%): the fault installed, and
produced nothing.** 12 of 12 synced, zero failures, and
`attempts_max = 0 / attempts_total = 0` - **no retry at all**. Compare M4
(503 + Retry-After, 50%), whose ledger does show `attempts_max = 1,
attempts_total = 2`.

So M2/M2t are either OpenLinker tolerating a malformed half without needing a
retry, or a fault landing on an endpoint this load path does not depend on for
order creation. **These artefacts cannot distinguish the two, so no resilience
claim is made for either arm.** Deciding it needs a per-request assertion that
the malformed response was actually consumed by the order path.

**M3 (quantity write rejected) created no orders at all**, which is correct
rather than a failure: its own observation says *"the quantity-write path
enqueues its jobs directly and pushes nothing at the stub"*. Its
`post_guard_attempts` answer (12 jobs with attempts>1) is recorded as a FAULT
observation, not a discard - see the next section.

## Why M3's verdict reads VALID while its observations record a DISCARDED line

Not a contradiction, and worth stating because it looks like one. F10 splits
`run_post_guards`' members into **STAND** guards, which still discard the
window, and **FAULT** guards, whose answer is written to
`fault-observations.txt` **as a measurement**. Under a deliberately injected
fault, retries are the expected observation rather than grounds for throwing
the window away. Its header also states which two of the eight do not run and
why (`generator_saturated` - no k6 on this path; `feed_starved` - F10 offers a
fixed batch on purpose, replaced by the better-targeted `offered_work`
observation).

I read this as a defect first and was wrong; recorded so the next reader does
not repeat it.

## I3 has no verdict, and the reason is an instrument defect

I3 `SIGKILL`s the worker 35 s into the window. Its window was refused by:

```
FATAL guard_scheduler_off: lab-worker-1 has OL_SCHEDULER_ENABLED=true
```

**The worker did not have that value. The worker was not running.** `lib.sh:567`
reads the posture as
`docker exec "$w" printenv OL_SCHEDULER_ENABLED 2>/dev/null || printf 'true'`,
so on a dead container the read fails, stderr is discarded, and the fallback
supplies `true`. Verified after the fact: `.env.lab` sets `false`, the compose
default is `false`, and the restarted worker reports `false`.

The **fail-closed direction is correct** - a guard that cannot read must not
certify. The **message is not**: it asserts a value it never read, which is
this campaign's catalogued "a probe that reads a proxy when an authority
exists". Had it said *could not read*, the remedy (restart the worker, re-run
the arm) would have been obvious instead of sending its reader after a phantom
scheduler. It bit precisely where it was most likely to - the one arm whose
purpose is to kill the worker.

## The stand was left broken, and how it was restored

F10's own restore warned, correctly and loudly, that it could not finish:

- `perf-prestashop.config.baseUrl` was left at `http://ps-fault-proxy:8080`
  while `lab-ps-fault-proxy` had already been removed - so every PrestaShop
  call would have failed for the next scenario. Restored to
  `http://prestashop` with its original `rateLimit` (60/min, 4 concurrent).
- `lab-api` was down (see above). Restarted; `/v1/health` answers 200.
- The stand lock released cleanly.

The restore could not run because it needs the api to `PATCH` the connection,
and the api was the thing I2 had killed. **A restore path that depends on the
component the scenario is allowed to kill cannot complete** - worth a
follow-up, since the warning text was the only thing that made the broken
state recoverable.

## What this run did not establish

- **I3 at all.** No verdict, no ledger comparison; the worker-kill arm is
  unmeasured and needs a re-run once the guard message or a worker-liveness
  precheck is fixed.
- **Whether M2/M2t demonstrate tolerance.** See above.
- **Whether a restarted api recovers I2's stalled work.** The observation
  ended with 12 jobs in flight and the api dead; nothing here measures what a
  restart would have drained.
- **Any figure at production scale.** 12 orders per window.
- **Any rate.** F10 measures behaviour under fault, never throughput.
