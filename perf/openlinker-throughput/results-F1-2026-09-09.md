All **measured**.

**The first two figures are knees; the third is not.** On both VALID
throughput arms the queue grew during the window - the system was offered more
than it took - so the sustained rate IS the service rate. On `lane-raised` the
queue did **not** grow: it drained the whole 900-order backlog inside the
window, so 10 658 orders/hour is a **floor on the ceiling**, not the ceiling.
Finding that arm's real knee needs a deeper backlog, and this run did not
measure it.

Those ratios are 8.5x, 12.3x and 36x, and **none of them may be published as a

latency-run1788744765                DISCARDED post_guard_limiter_degraded: 39 degraded-mode log line(s)
throughput-baseline-run1788746656    DISCARDED post_guard_limiter_degraded: 34  + destination_creates: 2 lack syncedAt

A degraded limiter has fallen back to per-process in-memory limiting, which

figures for this system - they are figures taken while the instrument the
system paces itself with was broken. The honest statement is **"a discarded
measurement versus a valid one"**, not a ratio.

## A correction to the brief's premise

The brief states the headline question as: *"`post_guard_destination_creates`
has since been repaired... Several windows were discarded **solely** by that
guard's unscoped predicate. Whether they now pass is the headline of this
run."*

Reading the four prior `verdict.txt` files, that premise does not hold:

1. **No window was discarded solely by it.** All four were discarded by
   `post_guard_limiter_degraded`; three were *additionally* discarded by
   `destination_creates`.
2. **That guard was already scoped when it refused.** Its own refusal text
   reads `2 lack syncedAt **on the declared destination**` - the scoped
   branch. So the refusal reported a real per-destination shortfall, not an
   artefact of an unscoped predicate.

What actually changed is narrower and better evidenced: **`post_guard_limiter_degraded`
answers `ok` on all four arms of this run** - zero degraded-mode entries,
against 34-52 per window before.

### And the cause of THAT is not isolated

`libs/shared/src/rate-limit/redis-rate-limiter.adapter.ts` did change between
the image the earlier runs used and this tree, but the diff is **log wording
only** (#2853's greppable tokens); no behaviour changed. So the limiter did not
learn to stop degrading.

The leading candidate is environmental. The limiter degrades when Redis is
unavailable *to it*, and the stand runs on a developer workstation alongside
two other OpenLinker docker stacks with no CPU pinning - a condition the
harness's own generated report states as a caveat on every figure it prints.
The earlier runs were taken while several agents and image builds contended
for that host; this one was taken at `load_average` 1.4-3.0.

That is a mechanism and a correlation. **It is not isolated, and no cause is
published for it here.**

## The one DISCARDED arm is the guard working, not a failed run

`throughput-lane-raised` was discarded on a guard no prior F1 arm reached:

```
DISCARDED post_guard_deferrals: 2 job(s) carry deferredTotalMs>0
```

A deferral is the penalty-free requeue (#2613/#2617) - a write refused because
a peer held the per-offer serialisation lock, or a destination throttled us.
Raising the lane caps raises concurrency, and more concurrency on one offer is
exactly what makes #2617's ordering guard defer a write. So the arm that
drained the fastest is also the arm that produced contention, and the guard
said so. Two deferred jobs out of a 900-order backlog.

This is worth stating plainly because it is the interesting shape in the
ladder: **the configuration that goes fastest is the one whose verdict this
guard chain refuses to certify.**

## A peer machine measured the SAME arms - do not average the two

A second machine (an EPYC server, branch
`2840-remeasure-epyc32-2026-09-09`) ran the same three throughput arms on the
same tree. **These are two different hosts and the figures must not be
averaged, pooled, or presented as a range.**

| Arm | this host (28-CPU WSL2 workstation) | peer host (EPYC server) | apart |
|---|---|---|---|
| `throughput-baseline` | **1 555 orders/hour** | **1 184 orders/hour** | 31% |
| `throughput-destination-raised` | 3 220 orders/hour | 3 162 orders/hour | 1.8% |
| `throughput-lane-raised` | 10 658 orders/hour (floor) | 10 983 orders/hour | 3.0% |

**The two tuned arms agree to within 2-3%, and the divergence is confined to
the factory-default arm.** That is the shape worth recording: once the
destination's own rate limit is raised, the two hosts measure the same system
to within run-to-run noise, so the tuned figures travel. The untuned figure
does not, and this report's 1 555/hour is this host's number, not the
platform's.

Why only the baseline arm diverges is **not established here**. It is the arm
whose pacing is tightest (manifest default, 60/min, 4 concurrent), so it is
the arm where a host difference has the most room to show - but that is a
mechanism, not a measurement, and isolating it would need both hosts to re-run
under a matched configuration.

## What this answers about real volume

5 000 orders/day is **208 orders/hour**. The baseline arm - the shipped
default connection rate limit, no tuning - sustains **1 555 orders/hour** on
this host as a measured knee, with a VALID verdict. **The peer host measured
1 184 orders/hour for the same arm**, so the defensible untuned claim is the
lower of the two: **at least 1 184 orders/hour**, which is still 5.7x the
5 000/day question. Raising only the destination's own rate limit takes both
hosts to ~3 200/hour.

**derived** from the measured knee above; the arithmetic is 1 555 / 208.

## What this run did not establish

- **The ceiling of the lane-raised configuration.** Its queue drained, so its
  figure is a floor. A deeper backlog is needed.
- **Why the limiter stopped degrading.** One mechanism, one correlation, not
  isolated.
- **Whether the 2 deferrals scale.** Two jobs at 900 orders says the condition
  exists, not how it grows.
- **Anything about a second destination or a second connection.** One
  PrestaShop destination, one Allegro-stub upstream.
- **A genuine marketplace round-trip.** The upstream is the stub; hop G's
  destination write is a real PrestaShop call, but the order source is not a
  real Allegro sandbox.
