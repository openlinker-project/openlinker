## What this VALID does and does not certify - a correction to my own pre-registration

Pre-registered prediction **P4** said F5 *"cannot produce a `VALID`/`DISCARDED`
verdict at all, since it does not call `run_post_guards`"*. **Half of that was
wrong.** F5 does not call `run_post_guards` and its `verdict.txt` carries **no
`guard=` lines at all** - that half held. But it writes its own verdict on its
own read-path criterion: every route request returned 2xx (1 449 of 1 449).

The distinction matters and is the reason to record it rather than quietly fix
the prediction: **an F5 `VALID` means "no route errored", not "eight
post-guards passed".** Reading it as the latter would overstate what it
certifies by seven guards.

## Dataset - measured at what was there, not reseeded

`order_records` **2 013 679** rows, of which **2 000 000** carry the
`perfseed_ord_` prefix. Run with `F5_ONLY_SIZE=1000000
F5_ONLY_LABEL=2M-actual`, deliberately: the scenario's default
`SIZES=(10000 100000 1000000)` would each no-op the additive seeder against a
2M table and then label three identical measurements 10K/100K/1M - which the
scenario's own comment warns about ("the default array would silently measure
a smaller label against a bigger table"). The seeder confirmed the no-op in
the log (`already at/above target: 2000000 ... nothing to do`) and the row
count is byte-identical before and after.

`F5_ONLY_SIZE` is passed to the seeder as `TARGET_ORDERS`, so it is set to
1 000 000 rather than the true 2 013 679 **on purpose**: the true figure is
above the 2 000 000 perfseed count and would have made the seeder insert the
13 679-row delta.

## The cost is the pagination COUNT, and `pg_stat_statements` names it

Top statement of the window by total time - 138 calls, 54.8 s total,
**397.0 ms mean**:

```sql
SELECT COUNT(*) FROM "order_records" "rec"
WHERE NOT (rec."recordStatus" = $2) AND NOT (rec."recordStatus" = $3)
  AND rec."syncStatus" @> $4::jsonb
```

That mean tracks the route's 384 ms p50, and the call count matches the
route's n exactly, so the route's cost **is** this count rather than merely
correlating with it.

The plain list is the same shape without the filter - 505 calls, **93.5 ms
mean** for a bare `COUNT(*)` over 2M rows - against its own 98.56 ms p50. So
both order-list routes are dominated by their pagination count, and the
`syncStatus @> jsonb` containment plus two `recordStatus` negations is what
makes one 4.2x the other.

## This is a known, already-decided condition - not a new finding

`decision-order-records-syncstatus-index-2026-09-06.md` decided **not** to add
a GIN index on `syncStatus` for this filter, measured against the same stand
at 1M rows, and recorded the decision in `order-record.repository.ts`
(`countByHealth`'s docblock) precisely so a future reader reaching for "just
add a GIN index" meets the measurement first.

That document frames the decision as *"not yet" rather than "never"*, revisitable
once #2943/#2957 moves the exact total off the synchronous request path. **This
run contributes one data point to that clause and nothing more.**

**What it does NOT contribute is a scaling claim.** The prior figure is
142-150 ms per execution at 1M rows; this run's is 397 ms mean at ~2M. Those
two numbers were produced by **different methods** - the earlier one by direct
`docker exec psql` against an otherwise idle table (the decision document
discloses this itself, along with the fact that no `lib.sh` guard ran for it),
this one from `pg_stat_statements` over 138 executions inside a live k6 window
with concurrent browse traffic. Concurrency alone could account for the
difference. **No super-linear scaling is claimed, and two points measured
differently cannot establish one.**

## What this run did not establish

- **Any scaling curve.** One dataset size, measured once. The three-size ladder
  the scenario is built for needs a stand seeded below 2M, and reseeding was
  out of scope.
- **Whether the needs-attention count is what real operators hit most.** The
  decision document names exactly this as the open product question its own
  measurement could not answer, and neither can this one.
- **Anything under write load.** Scheduler off, runner idle for this scenario;
  the reads competed only with each other.
- **The post-guard questions.** F5 runs none of them - see the correction at
  the top.
