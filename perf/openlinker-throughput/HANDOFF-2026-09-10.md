## 3. Product changes that stand, each with its evidence

1. **`fiscal` lane caps 2/1 -> 8/4** (`sync-job.runner.ts`). #3006's sweep:
   4.3x / 3.1x / 3.0x. ADR-050 § Amendment (#2840) records that per-scope 4 is
   measured and the total of 8 is derived.
2. **eparagony token reuse** (`eparagony-adapter.factory.ts`). The client
   cached its OAuth token correctly and `getCapabilityAdapter` built a fresh
   one per job, so the cache never survived: **22 token requests for 22
   documents**, counted at the stub. Now memoised per connection, keyed on
   `updatedAt` **and** a digest of the resolved credentials - `updateCredentials`
   does not touch the connection row, so a key built on `updatedAt` alone
   serves a rotated-away secret until restart. Verified: `tokens=1 creates=11`,
   +23% throughput.
3. **A disabled connection defers instead of retrying** (`resolveDeferral`).
   Was burning the whole ten-attempt ladder on a condition no attempt can
   change. **Known cost, discovered afterwards:** a disabled connection with a
   large backlog now holds it `queued` for the 24 h deferral budget instead of
   draining to `dead`, which blocks `guard_queue_empty` on a perf stand.
   Production surfaces it as `deferredCount`; the harness does not.

Also fixed: `adapter.types.spec` was red on the epic branch (#3043 added
`ShippingProviderManager` to `CoreCapabilityValues` and updated neither the
test nor the arch doc).

## 4. Withdrawn - do not resurrect without new evidence

- **`config.statusPollInitialDelayMs`** shipped and was reverted the same day.
  Measured **+1 016 ms and zero gain**: the poll loop reads FIRST and sleeps
  after, so the gap sits BETWEEN reads and can never remove the premature
  first one. The stub also cannot validate any poll-timing change - its
  readiness is `pollCount <= pollsUntilOutcome`, a COUNT, not elapsed time.
  A time-driven stub is the prerequisite for revisiting this.
- **"Fiscalization costs 114x more of our own time than invoicing."** Compared
  a synchronous HTTP call against a polling loop's wall clock.
- **"An Erli stock write reports success having written nothing."** Three
  stacked harness faults, not a product defect. F18 is VALID.
- **"8 323 ms per fiscal registration."** The mean included arm C's refused
  registration (20 ms, never crossed the provider boundary). Honest: 9 071 ms
  (n=11, spread 32 ms).
- **"600 webhook requests/s."** That was the OFFERED rate in a filename. The
  measured peak is 533.7/s.

## 5. Known contamination - read before quoting any figure from today

`.sampler.pid` holds one pid per directory. `window_start` starts a sampler,
and a scenario starting its own narrower one on the same directory overwrote
that pid and left the first sampler unstoppable. `SAMPLE_INTERVAL_SECS`
defaults to **1**, and `sample_queue` aggregates over `sync_jobs` - queries
F3's own `pg_stat_statements` capture timed at 390 ms and 1 300 ms mean.

Found alive: an F18 sampler that had run **2 h 58 m**, through F14, F15, F16,
F17 and all three F11 attempts. Fixed in `lib.sh` (`sampler_start` now
replaces and warns).

Four arms were re-measured quiet and moved **under 1%** (§ 2), because each is
bound by a declared stub latency rather than by Postgres. **A figure bound by
database work has no such immunity and was not re-taken** - that is F5's read
path, F1's ingestion ladder and F3's webhook burst.

An earlier claim in the record that F14's window was "provably clean" because
it held exactly twelve `sync_jobs` rows is **wrong about the right thing**: a
leaked sampler creates no jobs, it issues SELECTs.

## 6. What is badly measured, and why

- **F8 / the `realtime` lane.** Four VALID arms and the lane still keeps 4/2
  and stays labelled illustrative. Per-job exec time is FLAT across caps 1-8
  and lowest at the widest cap, which reads like licence to raise it and is
  not: the destination latency was the fallback 120 ms with
  `quantityMsMeasured: false` in every manifest, only one latency was swept
  where the scenario's own falsifiable claim needs two, and throughput goes
  super-linear 4 -> 8 because 240 jobs at ~110 ms drain in 20 s and fixed
  overhead dominates. See `results-F8-2026-09-10.md` § 4 for what would close
  it.
- **F11 arm 2** produces 2 328/h and is DISCARDED on 4 of ~194 jobs retrying
  with `Missing mapping for order item productRef` - the ordinary self-healing
  `awaiting_mapping` gap, appearing as a warm-up while the source connection's
  product mappings are minted. The figure is a usable LOWER BOUND with that
  stated. **Fix is a pre-window mapping warm-up, never a looser guard.**
- **Every stub latency is a declared constant, never a distribution.** Nothing
  here measures inFakt, KSeF, Subiekt, InPost, DPD, the real eparagony.pl or a
  real Allegro/Erli rate limit. Those are separate unknown numbers and none is
  derivable from these runs.

## 8. Stand hygiene that bit repeatedly

- **Never edit a running scenario file.** Bash reads scripts incrementally; an
  insertion mid-run killed F11 arm 3 with `line 453: indow_stop: command not
  found`. This happened twice in this campaign.
- **`guard_queue_empty` will refuse** to open a window over a previous run's
  backlog. Drain with a targeted `DELETE FROM sync_jobs WHERE status IN
  ('queued','running') AND "connectionId" = ...`.
- **Check for stray scenario processes before measuring**: `ps -eo pid,etime,cmd
  | grep 'scenarios/f'`. Match by PID, never `pkill -f` - that pattern matches
  your own shell and has killed it here.
- **Two TLS terminators cache their upstream address** (`lab-eparagony-stub-tls`,
  `lab-erli-stub-tls`). Recreating a backing stub makes them 502 silently
  forever; `guard_connection_endpoints` now catches it, restart the terminator.
- **F11's teardown deliberately disables `perf-prestashop-source`** (no DELETE
  endpoint exists). `f11_ensure_source_connection` re-enables it now.
- **`OL_API_URL` defaults to :13000 in `lib.sh`** and this stand publishes the
  api on `:19000`. Export it.

## 9. The pattern worth carrying forward

Eight of the eleven defects fixed today were not slow code. They were
**instruments that lied**: a verdict reporting VALID over an arm that scored
0/10, another over 5 of 8 failed publishes, `sampler_stop` reporting a stop
while the sampler kept polling, five manifests recording `gitSha: unknown`, a
proxy 502-ing in silence, a default stub tenant that never existed, a scenario
measuring a connection it had itself disabled, and a flat exec-time column that
reads as permission to raise a cap.

Prefer a target an existing COUNTER already measures. The two product changes
that survived contact with measurement were both found that way; the one that
did not was found by reading code and reasoning about it.
