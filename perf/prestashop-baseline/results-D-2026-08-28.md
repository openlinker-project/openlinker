# Results D - timing and throughput at 100 000 products (#2644)

Run 2026-08-27/28 on `epic/2590-prestashop-no-module` @ `8e6b2081d`. This asks a different
question from `results-C`: not "did the epic help" but "do its bounds still hold an order
of magnitude above the 10 000 products they were sized against". A regression here is a
sizing finding, not a failure of `results-C`'s comparison, and both stay valid.

## The stand

| | |
|---|---|
| Machine | one host, 28 logical CPUs, 15 GB RAM, Docker on WSL2 |
| **Worker replica count** | **1** (single `ol-perf-worker`, `OL_WORKER_ROLE=jobs`) |
| Lane caps in force | `realtime=4/2 bulk=12/8 fiscal=2/1 fan-out=8/4` |
| Container set | `ol-demo-fresh-{api,postgres,redis,mysql,prestashop,woocommerce,woocommerce-mysql}` + `ol-perf-worker` |
| Network | localhost only, no tunnel, no VPN |

**Lane caps bound one worker PROCESS.** Every cap figure is per process; N replicas
multiply each effective cap by N.

## Honesty ledger for this report

- Figures are labelled **measured**, **derived** (arithmetic on measured values) or
  **extrapolated**. A full-cycle time at this catalogue size cannot be sat through and is
  always derived.
- 10 000-product figures are never restated as if they applied at 100 000.
- A bound that was not reached is reported as **not exercised**, never as holding.
- Store impact is measured with the status-code-checking probe, and this report says so.

## Bound 2 - the two read ceilings (#2608) - ANSWERED, but three of four scans are NOT EXERCISED

`PRESTASHOP_NARROWED_MAX_ROWS = 5000`, `PRESTASHOP_UNNARROWED_MAX_ROWS = 50000`; the read
throws `PrestashopTruncatedReadException` rather than truncating.

Row counts measured in the shop's own MySQL:

| collection | at 10 000 products | read shape | verdict |
|---|---:|---|---|
| `ps_product_attribute` (combinations) | 30 008 | narrowed to 100 products/page (~300 rows) | safe at any size |
| `ps_stock_available` | 40 025 | narrowed per product | safe at any size |
| packs (`cache_is_pack = 1`) | **0** | un-narrowed enumeration | **NOT EXERCISED** |
| `ps_category` | **9** | un-narrowed scan | **NOT EXERCISED** |
| `ps_feature` / `ps_feature_value` | 2 / 10 | un-narrowed scan | **NOT EXERCISED** |
| `ps_attribute_group` / `ps_attribute` | 5 / 30 | un-narrowed scan | definitions only |

Template product 22 has `cache_is_pack = 0`, so the synthetic catalogue mints **no packs at
any size**. The un-narrowed pack enumeration, the category scan and the feature scan are
therefore not exercised by this catalogue and this run says nothing about them. Only a
catalogue with real product variety can.

The combinations prefetch is the one that looked risky and is not: it is narrowed to a page
of 100 products, so ~300 rows, three orders of magnitude under its 50 000 budget.

## Bound 5 - outbox cap and drain (#2604 / #2614) - static answer

| constant | value |
|---|---:|
| `RETENTION_MAX_ROWS` | 100 000 |
| `RETENTION_DELETE_BATCH_SIZE` | 1 000 |
| `RETENTION_MAX_BATCHES_PER_PASS` | 10 -> at most 10 000 deletions per pass |

`backlog_over_cap = ($stillOver && $exhausted)` **cannot fire spuriously by construction**:
it requires the table to be genuinely still over cap AND the pass to have spent all ten
delete batches. An overshoot above 10 000 rows converges over several passes and reports
the flag while it does.

**#2614's AC3 confirmed absent**, which is why that issue is left open: `DeliveryRunner.php`
contains no wall-clock budget of any kind - no deadline, no `microtime` check, no per-run
time limit. The epic's statement about it is accurate.

## Bound 7 - the per-connection sync-status read (#2615) - MEASURED

`GET /v1/connections/:id/sync-status`, five consecutive calls at **33 537** `sync_jobs` rows:

    16.4, 10.9, 8.2, 7.8, 7.9 ms   (all HTTP 200)

The read is two bounded aggregates plus one single-row cursor read: the live-queue aggregate
is restricted to `status IN ('queued','running')` so it scales with the live queue rather
than with history, and the historical aggregate is bounded by a 7-day `createdAt` window.
`averageAttemptDurationMs` correctly reported `null` with `attemptDurationSampleSize: 0`,
because `lastAttemptDurationMs` is NULL on every row predating its migration and the read
excludes NULL rather than counting it as zero.

### Re-measured at the 100 000-product stand

`sync_jobs` held **30 933** rows (23 687 for this connection). Five calls:

    19.6, 9.3, 7.9, 13.0, 7.8 ms   (all HTTP 200)

Unchanged. `averageAttemptDurationMs` now reports **12 729 ms over 178 samples**, so
#2611's column is populated end to end by the epic worker and the read's non-null sample
size is doing its job (12.7 s is a batch child hydrating 100 products, which is the right
order of magnitude).

**Honest limit on this answer.** The history did not actually grow during the campaign -
it fell from 33 537 to 30 933, because every scenario runner purges the connection's
queue. So this measures the read at ~31 000 rows, NOT at "100 000 products with a full job
history", which is what the bound asks about. What can be said from the design rather than
the measurement: the live-queue aggregate is restricted to `status IN ('queued','running')`
and the historical one to a 7-day `createdAt` window, so the cost scales with the live
queue and one week of history rather than with total rows - and `sync_jobs` has no
retention anywhere in the tree, so that bounding is the only thing standing between this
panel and an unbounded scan. **Not exercised at a large history; reported as such.**

## Seeding, and why AC1 cannot be met in one sitting

90 000 products added at `SEED_OFFSET=10000` (ordinals 10001..100000), **in 38 seconds** -
the seed is one set-based `INSERT ... SELECT`, not a row-by-row loop. MySQL went
651 -> 722 MB and did not OOM; the documented exit-137 trap did not fire, and disk was
never a factor (857 GB free, the shop schema was 50 MB at 10 000 products).

| | PrestaShop | OpenLinker |
|---|---:|---:|
| products | **100 000** | 2 878 mapped |
| combinations | 300 008 | 8 526 variants |
| stock rows | 400 025 | |

**#2644's AC1 - 100 000 confirmed on BOTH sides before any figure is taken - could not be
met, and the reason is bound 1 itself.** A full catalogue replication cycle at this size is
~2.8 days (derived). Staging the verification the issue asks for would take days of
unattended running. That is reported as the finding rather than as a setup failure: after
this epic, a 100 000-product shop still takes the better part of three days to replicate
once, and the two sweeps that did NOT get a bigger budget take 10 and 42 days.

Consequence for reading the runs below: a page whose products are already mapped measures
steady state, a page whose products are not measures the identifier-mapping CREATE branch.
Both are legitimate and they are different numbers, so each run states which it was.

## Bound 6 - requests per SKU at 100 000 - MEASURED, and it does not move

| run | elapsed | requests | products | per SKU | mappings created |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 (cold caches, discarded) | 56 s | 35 | 500 | 0.070 | 0 |
| 2 | **46 s** | **29** | 500 | **0.058** | 0 |
| 3 | **46 s** | **29** | 500 | **0.058** | 0 |

Zero retries, every response 200. **Median of runs 2-3: 29 requests, 46 s, 500 products.**

| | 10 000 products | 100 000 products |
|---|---:|---:|
| requests per tick | 29 | **29** |
| elapsed per tick | 46 s | **46 s** |
| requests per SKU | 0.058 | **0.058** |

Measured, and identical to the request. **Requests per SKU does not change with catalogue
size**, because a tick covers a fixed 500 products whatever the catalogue holds - #2593's
budget is per run, not per catalogue. The epic's caches survive the jump: the working set
that matters is one page of 100 products, not the catalogue.

Run 1's extra 6 requests are the cold-start reads (2 `product_options`,
2 `product_option_values`, 1 `configurations`, 1 `currencies/<id>`) after the worker
restart, once per resolver per TTL. Notably that is 2 and 1 rather than the 8 seen in
`results-C` A3: the batch children start staggered enough that fewer of them miss the
cache simultaneously, so the stampede is real but its size depends on arrival timing.

### The late page, and the create branch

`run-a1a.sh` clears the sweep cursor before every run, so on its own it only ever measures
**offset 0**. `run-a1a-at-offset.sh` seeds the cursor to a chosen offset and verifies the
read-back before enqueuing - the first version of that script silently failed to seed
(it wrote a `cursorValue` column that does not exist; the real one is `value`) and
measured offset 0 while claiming offset 99 000. The seed is now verified and a failure is
fatal, because a probe that lies about where it measured is worse than no probe.

| page | requests | elapsed | products | mappings created | proof |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| offset 0, already mapped | 29 | 46 s | 500 | 0 | ids `20..100497` |
| **offset 99 000, unmapped** | **30** | **46 s** | 500 | **500** | ids `208999..209198`, cursor advanced to `:99500` |

Measured. Two things follow, and both are good news for the epic:

**Offset paging does not degrade.** A page 99 000 rows into a 100 000-product catalogue
costs one more request than the first page and exactly the same wall clock. The
`limit=99000,500` read is not a scan the shop struggles with at this size.

**The identifier-mapping create branch is nearly free on the wire.** The late page created
500 new mappings - it took the create path in full - for **+1 request and +0 seconds**.
The create branch is database work, not platform requests, so a first replication costs
the shop essentially the same as a re-sync. That is worth knowing because `results-A`'s
trap list warns that a cold run "takes a different and more expensive code path"; at the
platform boundary, measured here, it does not.
