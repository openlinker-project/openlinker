#2823 · round 5 · variant-centric /listings

One row per variant, one strip of the channels it is on

Reproduces the hand-drawn lo-fi with components that already ship. Four rules govern every frame below: every mark carries its own name, an error is the only red, a gap is the only accent, and the row's height never depends on how many connections the operator has. Every figure in the file is derived from a single fixture, so a number quoted in one frame is the same number in the next.

Round 4 removed a variant. Rounds 1–3 carried two designs side by side — a grid with one column per connection, and this strip. The grid is gone (PM decision, 3 September 2026): it ran out of container width at the tenth connection, and past the header its marks stopped saying which channel they belonged to. Frames 02 and 03 keep the measurement and the reasoning, because the ceiling is worth knowing about; nothing in this sheet draws the grid any more, and the choice is settled rather than open.

Reading the marks. † marks a state the design needs and today's data cannot produce — drawn as a proposal, never as a screenshot of something that works. Every measurement printed in frames 01 and 02 is taken by the page from itself at render time, so it cannot drift away from the picture above it.

02 The row does not grow when the operator adds a channel the scrolled header · then the same fixture at 3 / 9 / 14 connections, measured

This is the property that does not show up in a screenshot of the top of the page. The panel above is scrolled past its own header — which is where an operator spends most of their time, because the table is ten rows long and the header is one — and nothing was lost, because no mark here ever depended on a column heading to say which connection it belongs to. This is the defect that killed the discarded variant: a grid of shapes reads "Active on Shopify" only while the word Shopify is on screen. Sticky headers would have fixed that and are not on the table — a decision, and one the system agrees with: DataTable has no pinned-header support at all, its only pinned element being the bottom footer rail (data-table.tsx:118-130).

This measurement was expected to report a cost and refused twice. The prediction was a trade: a grid runs out of width, a strip runs out of height, pick your poison. The width half happened exactly as predicted, which is why the grid is gone — every connection cost 92 px of a container that has about 1220, so the tenth column left the page and from there the answer was sideways scrolling, 459 px of it at fourteen. The height half never turned up. The first draft put the gaps in the strip too and its worst row grew four pixels. Then the gaps moved into the expansion (PM decision), and the readouts above now say the same number three times: 64 px at three connections, 64 px at nine, 64 px at fourteen.

The reason is worth stating because it is the load-bearing part of the design rather than a lucky fixture: the strip is bounded by how many offers a variant HAS, not by how many connections the operator has. A variant lives on a handful of channels — the longest strip anywhere in this fixture is five pills — and adding a fifteenth connection adds nothing to a row that is not listed on it. A grid is the mirror image: it draws a cell per connection whether or not anything is there, so its width is a function of the operator's setup and grows whether or not the catalogue does.

Which leaves one real cost, and it is not a pixel count. B answers "where is this?" and answers "where is it not?" only by absence. A channel missing from the strip is a channel the variant is not on — true, and how /products already reads, but it asks the operator to hold their own connection list in their head. At three connections that is nothing; at fourteen, noticing that Kaufland is absent from a strip of five is harder than seeing a hole in a column. This was the discarded grid's one advantage, and round 5 answers it rather than living with it: the Coverage column states the same fact as a number — 2/3, 5/9, 5/14 — so "how much of my estate does this reach?" is readable without counting pills, and only then does the strip say which ones. It does not make absence locatable; it makes it countable, which is the half that a strip cannot do for itself. Reading a low number is now the prompt to expand the row, where every missing connection is named as a button.

03 Every mark carries its own name what each mark is, and which shipped class draws it

The vocabulary got shorter by getting more specific. The discarded grid had to ration words: a column sized on the widest one it might ever contain sets the table's ceiling, so Invalid was let in and Draft, Ended and Not synced shared one hollow dot and were told apart only in the expansion. A strip pays no such rent — its width comes from the remainder, not from its longest label — so the lifecycle word rides on the pill and the three states that used to share a shape now say which one they are. The shipped tone rule is unchanged either way: listing-row-state.ts:150-183 tones all three neutral with the comment "soft, like Ended and Not synced", and only Invalid is toned error, because only Invalid asks for work.

One figure a reviewer will look for, recorded rather than left to be discovered: the channel dot inside .channel-pill measures 2.56:1 against the pill's own fill in light mode (5.71:1 dark), under the 3:1 that 1.4.11 asks of a meaningful graphic. It stays. It is shipped CSS rendered on /products today, this file transcribes rather than edits the system, and in this composition the dot is decoration — the channel is named in text right beside it at 8.88:1, so nothing is carried by the dot alone. If the dot ever becomes the only identifier of a channel, that is the moment the figure matters.

04 The expansion is today's table, one level down — and it still navigates the shipped six columns, plus the two that lose their host · gaps under the table

Listing and Status are not new columns. Today both live inside the identity cell — the external offer id as .listing-cell__offerid, the lifecycle as a StatusBadge beside the product name — and when the identity cell moves up to the parent row they need somewhere to go. Everything else is the shipped column set verbatim, including the two headings that name whose number they carry: price and quantity are what the CHANNEL reports, already through the connection's pricing rule and already net of its stock buffer (#1843 / #1844), and Updated is when OL last READ the channel, not when the listing changed.

Two things moved in round 4, and both put back something the demotion took away. The rows navigate: today's /listings table passes rowHref={(m) => m.id} (listings-list-page.tsx:918), so its rows already open the mapping card, and round 3 dropped that when it moved the table down a level. It is a regression being repaired, not a feature being added. And the gap buttons moved below the table: the expansion is a statement of where this variant IS, so opening it with a list of places it is not put a form where a status belonged.

The split between the two clickable things is not a choice — DataTable makes it for us. "Expandable takes precedence over rowHref for the row-level click" (data-table.tsx:90), and linkifyFirstCell is Boolean(href) && !expandable (:472). A row cannot both expand and navigate. So the outer variant row expands, the inner listing row navigates, and neither could take the other's job even if we wanted it to. The anchor sits on the first cell only, for the same reason: that is where the component puts it.

05 Where a listing row goes /listings/:id — the page that already ships, drawn only far enough to prove the trip

Nothing here is designed. /listings/:id exists (listings.route.tsx:51-58) and ListingDetailPage already renders this exact header — eyebrow="Listings" over title={`Mapping — ${mapping.externalId}`}, with a back link to /listings (listing-detail-page.tsx:124-136) — over a KeyValueList of the same eight fields in the same order, five of them mono (:139-161). This frame is a transcription, and it is here for one reason: an expansion whose rows click through to nowhere is a design that has not been checked.

Three sections of the real page are deliberately not drawn (PM decision): the marketplace offer section, the Offer creation block with its status and validator errors, and the Context raw-payload panel — plus the Edit offer action. #2823 does not change that page, and redrawing a finished screen invites a reviewer to review it.

Two fields are honest fabrications and are marked as such in the source. Created and Updated have no fixture field behind them — the fixture models a channel status read (legitimately never for an unsynced offer), which is a different fact from the mapping row's own timestamps — so they are stable filler rather than a borrowed column. Entity Type is not a constant: it follows the connection's kind, because a marketplace connection carries Offer mappings and a shop connection carries ShopProduct (architecture-overview § Listings). Expand a WooCommerce row in frame 01 and the card says so.

06 The tab counts offers, the row is a variant All is new and default · a filtered row shows which cell let it in

Two things worth deciding with open eyes. First, the number stops being the row count: the counts come from lifecycleCounts, which the API returns under the same search and connection filters but never narrowed by the lifecycle itself (listings.types.ts:216-227), so they still count offers. Second, today a badge is suppressed when the tab already states it (activeLifecycle) — here the pill IS the mark, so suppressing it would blank the thing the row is made of, and the rule is not ported. The matching pill takes an outline instead, because a strip of five pills does not say by itself which one answered the filter.

07 What the gap does when you click it in flight † · landed · refused · the rows are drawn expanded, because that is where the buttons are

The middle state is the honest one and it surprises people: a freshly published offer lands as Not synced, not Active. The mapping row is written the moment createOffer returns, but the lifecycle is derived from a channel status read that has not happened yet — so the pill saying Not synced is correct, and a design promising a live-looking mark on success would be promising something the pipeline does not deliver. The in-flight state † has nothing behind it at all: the mapping does not exist while the job runs, so there is no row to mark pending. use-bulk-batch-query (poll 5 s, auto-stop) is the reusable piece if we decide to splice progress in from the batch entity.

08 Before there is a grid, and when there cannot be one loading · no connections · empty catalogue · error · read-only

Zero connections is its own state, not an empty table: a grid whose columns come from connections has no columns at all, and "no listings found" would read as a broken feature rather than "connect a channel to use this page". The skeleton reserves the row height it will actually get, which for this table is the two-line identity — declare it with DataTableColumn.lines on the column that sets the height, per #2538.

09 768 px — the strip wraps, the row grows iPad on the shop floor

Same table, narrower container — and because the strip is the column that takes the remainder, what gives is row height rather than the right-hand edge. That is the whole trade this design made, seen at the width where it first shows: the identity column keeps its 22rem floor, the strip gets what is left, and a variant on four channels wraps to a second line sooner than it would at 1440. Nothing is hidden with hideBelow, and there is nothing left to freeze: a fixed-layout table does not scroll sideways, so there is no column that could slide out from under the identity.

10 360 px — the grid stops being a grid triage from a phone, off-hours

A table cannot exist at 360 px, so the card reshapes around the same priority the row has — except inverted: gaps first, named and tappable, then the marks for everything that is listed. That inversion is deliberate and is the one place this sheet contradicts itself on purpose. On the desktop row the listed pills lead and the gaps live under the expansion, because the expansion is a statement of state; on a phone there is no expansion to put them in, and off-hours triage is looking for the hole. This is DataTable's own card branch, and the reshape is deliberate rather than the row squeezed — the identity leads, the channel names come back as words because there is no column header left to carry them.

What has to exist before this can be built

  1. A read model that returns absence. Neither GET /listings (enumerates mappings, sorts hard-coded createdAt DESC, id DESC, no sort parameter) nor GET /products (aggregates at product level) can answer "one row per variant with the holes visible". The shape needed is variant × connection, and the holes are the payload.
  2. A productId, variantId sort key. The AC's "a product's variants appear consecutively" is the only thing flat rows still need from the backend. /products already has a sort/dir pair as precedent; none of its values groups by product.
  3. Per-connection publish eligibility on the row. The gap's affordance is decided by publishDestinationKind() — OfferCreator, or enabled ProductPublisher — which the FE can resolve from the connections read it already makes. Nothing new server-side, but it has to reach the row: it decides whether a gap renders as a button or as a named, unactionable pill.
  4. An in-flight publication read † — either persist the mapping earlier, or read OfferCreationRecords and splice them in. This is also what makes a cell refresh without a reload.
  5. A batch that carries more than one destination †, if bulk publish is ever wanted here. BulkListingBatch.connectionId is a scalar column and the wizard route reads a single connectionId: migration plus a new request shape, reaching the at-most-once advancement counters (#737). Not in this design — recorded because the repo has no note of this scope ever being asked for.
  6. A --channel-* token per new channel. Six exist. Everything else renders the grey fallback dot, which #2028 already had to fix once for Erli and WooCommerce.

What round 4 settled, and the one thing it did not

Settled, 3 September 2026. The column grid is rejected and no longer drawn anywhere in this sheet — do not reopen the choice in a later round. Rounds 1–3 also filed a follow-up issue against it ("choose how the column set survives past ten connections", with a priced menu of four mitigations); that question has no subject any more and is withdrawn rather than left dangling. The ceiling measurement survives in frame 02 as the reason.

Also settled: the gap buttons sit below the expansion's table, not above it, and the expansion's rows navigate to /listings/:id. The second of those is a regression being repaired — today's /listings rows already navigate (rowHref, listings-list-page.tsx:918) and round 3 lost it when it demoted that table a level — so a dev reading this should expect to keep the shipped behaviour, not to write it.

Also settled, and it reverses an earlier cut: the Coverage column. A per-row count over connections (2/3, 5/9, 5/14) sits between the variant and the strip. It was the design's one open question for three rounds — the strip answers "where is this?" and answers "where is it not?" only by absence, which is the one advantage the rejected grid had — and it is not the issue's own Allegro 1/1 badge coming back: that counted a single connection, where the answer is binary and the number was "yes in costume". This counts connections.

One decision it leaves open, and a dev should not resolve it quietly. The denominator counts every connection in view, including one that structurally cannot accept a listing — publishDestinationKind() resolving to nothing, i.e. no OfferCreator and no enabled ProductPublisher. Such a variant can never read 14/14, and 13/14 looks like a gap no action can close. The cell's hover names the shortfall rather than the denominator hiding it, because a denominator that quietly excluded connections would make two rows showing the same number mean different things. Excluding them is a product decision, not an implementation detail.
