The Rule Composer

An operator is not filling in a row of a condition table. They are writing one sentence about their market — and they have to read it back before saving, because the engine has no priorities and holds the order when two rules match. This epic makes the sentence visible, the amount typeable, and three live misconfigurations unreachable.

01

What is there now

Reproduced from the running instance, flaws included. The modal knows everything about its own fields and nothing about what the rule will do.

Eight things to fix

  • 1The threshold is cut to Selec…. Neither amount, nor currency, nor full name — the operator chooses blind.
  • 2An amount cannot be typed. Only a pick from seeded thresholds, so a limit of your own is inexpressible.
  • 3AND only — and that stays. The problem is not the missing OR, it is the cost of writing the second rule. See §3.
  • 4Condition columns have no labels. Three selects in a row and you guess which is the fact, which the test, which the value.
  • 5A footnote written for developers. "one closed, cross-country vocabulary" is a sentence about our implementation.
  • 6A tax-ID checkbox, disabled, explaining what an adapter cannot do — and unnecessary either way. See §5.
  • 7Three shouting orange headers. The brand accent should be rare; here it fires three times in one modal.
  • 8Dates as mm/dd/yyyy in a product adjudicating Polish and German markets — 09/10/2026 means two different things.

03

Instead of OR — a second rule

A retraction, kept on the record An earlier draft drew an "any of" group. Withdrawn. The flagship case — under 450 PLN or 100 EUR — already works as two rules, and it is the currency mismatch that guarantees they cannot both fire. An OR destroys that guarantee: one rule matches on whichever branch evaluates, and the other branch's mismatch is swallowed.

The euro rule routes to a different kind and a different provider — which is exactly what a second rule buys and an alternative inside one rule gives away. The warning at the bottom is the third live defect: today a rule can name a connection that has opted out of being a destination, and nothing says so.

04

Two live misconfigurations, made unreachable

Both were found on the running instance. Neither is a rendering problem — each is a shape that should not have been expressible.

Defect — both country defaults set

Two independent selects. Set both and the step is disabled entirely — an unmatched order is held rather than taking either default. The product detects it and explains it afterwards.

Fix — one select, one outcome

S4

Defect — one country, two markets

/settings/sales-documents

PL with 16 orders and 3 rules, and pl with 1 order and 0 rules. The country code is compared case-sensitively somewhere between ingestion and market discovery, so an order can land in a market that has no rules and is silently held while the operator looks at a fully configured card.

Decision inside this epic Normalise on write and new orders are fixed while the existing "pl" market is stranded with its one order; normalise on read and they merge, but nothing records that anything was ever wrong. Either way the stranded market needs a disposition — it is not enough to stop creating new ones.
05

The tax-ID checkbox, removed

There is no screen to show here, and that is the entire change.

off
Include the buyer's tax ID on the receipt, where the destination supports it

A property of the Receipt outcome, not a separate document type. Not workable today — eparagony.pl's adapter has no tax-id field yet.

Gone. No toggle, because there is no choice to make.

Why it dissolves rather than moves If the order has a tax ID, it goes to the adapter. Full stop. No switch, because there is no decision; no capability gate on the destination, because the adapter decides what to do with it. The value travels with the order like the currency does — so no property is needed on the rule or on the routing decision. Customer tax ID survives purely as a condition.

What this epic deliberately does not do

  • No rule priorities. One field would resolve every collision — but a silent win of one rule over another, for a fiscal document, is exactly the adjudication this domain avoids. Holding with a reason is worse for throughput and better for the operator.
  • No OR, no NOT. The first draft drew "any of" groups; withdrawn. A separate rule gives what an alternative cannot: its own document kind and its own destination.
  • No change to the closed fact vocabulary. Customer tax ID, delivery country, order total — three facts. The developer footnote leaves the screen; the principle stays.
  • No rule naming. Cut after audit — invented scope, and the only thing that coupled Epic 1 to this one.
